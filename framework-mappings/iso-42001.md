# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 mapping

**Source:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023, *Information technology — Artificial
intelligence — Management system*. Annex A reference control objectives A.2–A.10.
**Checked:** 3 September 2026 · **Mapping version:** 0.1.0

ISO/IEC 42001 specifies an **AI management system** — an organisational
framework, certifiable by an accredited body. Decirance is not a management
system and cannot make anyone conformant with one. What it can do is produce
evidence that an AI management system needs: a per-deployment record of what
was assessed, on what basis, by whom, and what changed since.

The mapping is therefore lopsided on purpose. Clauses 4–10 (context,
leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement)
are organisational and Decirance addresses almost none of them. Annex A is where
the correspondence is real.

Annex A is **informative**, not normative — organisations select controls
against their own risk profile — so this maps to control objective groups
rather than asserting individual control conformance.

## Annex A control objectives

| Group | Heading | Addressed by | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.2** | Policies related to AI | Red lines; permit policy | `prohibited_actions` in the Context Contract; a breach caps the recommendation at reject regardless of other evidence |
| **A.3** | Internal organization | Accountable owner; field guide roles | Every permit names who accepted the residual risk. The field guide records, per claim, which role is positioned to answer for it |
| **A.4** | Resources for AI systems | Agent Passport | An inventory of the model, prompt, tools, permissions, data sources, MCP servers, environment and residency — hashed, so "what resources" has a definite answer at a point in time |
| **A.5** | Assessing impacts of AI systems | — | **Not covered.** There is no impact assessment in this framework. The Assurance Pack records residual risk accepted by a named person, which is a decision record, not an impact assessment |
| **A.6** | AI system life cycle | Permit lifecycle; assurance delta | The 10-state permit machine, plus the change→invalidation→targeted reassessment→reissue loop. This is the strongest correspondence in the mapping |
| **A.7** | Data for AI systems | C-03, C-10, C-11, C-13 | Retrieval scope, retention and residency terms, source allowlisting with a named owner, memory writer control and rollback |
| **A.8** | Information for interested parties | Assurance Pack; Technical Pack | Two audiences, deliberately separated: a decision record for the accountable owner, and the underlying claims, evidence, hazards and graph for a reviewer |
| **A.9** | Use of AI systems | Context Contract; permit invariant | The authority granted is explicit and bounded, and `checkPermitInvariant` decides whether a given action is within it. Every clause fails closed |
| **A.10** | Third-party and customer relationships | C-11, C-14; commercial change kinds | MCP and model supply chain; provider, plan and terms changes are classified and invalidate the claims that rest on them |

## Clauses 4–10

Not mapped. These are management-system requirements — scope definition,
leadership commitment, competence, internal audit, management review,
continual improvement — and they are properties of an organisation, not of a
tool. Decirance produces inputs to clause 9 (performance evaluation) in the
form of permit event history and reassessment records, and nothing more.

Anyone using this to support a 42001 certification should treat it as evidence
for specific Annex A objectives, produced by a tool, and not as a shortcut
through the clauses.

## What this mapping does not claim

It does not claim conformance or certification, and it is not endorsed by ISO,
IEC, or any certification body. ISO/IEC 42001 is a copyrighted standard; this
document paraphrases the Annex A group headings for identification and does not
reproduce its text. Buy the standard.

**A.5, assessing impacts of AI systems, is uncovered**, and that is worth
naming rather than burying: a framework that reasons carefully about whether
evidence justifies operation, and not at all about who is harmed if it does, is
incomplete in a way that matters to a 42001 auditor.
